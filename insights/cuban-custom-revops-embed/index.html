Cuban Is Right About Custom. Wrong About Who Does It for RevOps.

Cuban is right that utilization wins. For RevOps, custom isn’t a feature flag. It’s an embed.

Michael Bogart · CEO & Chief Revenue Architect, Revenue-Growth.AI · September 20, 2026

Mark Cuban said: “Software is dead because everything’s gonna be customized to your unique utilization.”

He’s naming a real problem. Most GTM software ignores it.

If you run RevOps at a Series A–C B2B SaaS company on Salesforce or HubSpot, you already know the gap. Your stages aren’t textbook. Your ownership rules are tribal. Your forecast fields carry history your vendors never saw. Your private CRM data is the utilization Cuban is talking about.

Generic GTM products were built for a category. Not for your process.

What doesn’t match utilization

Two patterns show up every time someone tries to “add AI” to GTM without touching the system of record.

Prompting and API wrappers. Slap a chatbot on your notes. Wire a “next best action” that never reads ownership, stage age, or the real definition of qualified. You get confident theater. Reps ignore it by week two.

Another point tool. New seats. New integrations. Same broken fields and handoffs underneath. Utilization doesn’t improve because the process still doesn’t match how you sell.

Neither path sits inside your CRM with an operator who will change the system. Both skip the part that hurts.

What “customized to your utilization” actually means in RevOps

It means the system behaves like your team sells — not like a product page.

That starts with private data in your account. Not a shared multi-tenant guess. Then process: stages, SLAs, routing, forecast logic that matches how you win. Only after that do agents make sense. Automate a broken process and you scale the break.

The sequence is short on purpose:

Data → processes → agents.

Skip step one, agents lie. Skip step two, agents nag. Do all three in someone else’s abstraction, and you’re back to generic software wearing an AI badge.

The motion that fits Series A–C

Most teams in the $5M–$50M band can’t (and shouldn’t) staff a permanent GTM systems org overnight. A full-time hire ramps for months. A DIY build turns your best operator into a part-time engineer. Another SaaS seat adds seats without fixing utilization.

There’s a cleaner option: a six-month AI GTM / RevOps embed.

An operator works in your stack. Cleanup first. Process second. Agents third. When a platform deploy fits — same motion, your environment — it’s included. When it doesn’t, the embed still stands. Standalone platform exists; it isn’t the lead SKU.

You’re not buying a demo. You’re buying temporary operator capacity that leaves the system working where your team already lives.

What to reject this quarter

  • Tools that promise AI without touching field quality or stage definitions
  • Wrappers that never sit inside your CRM with an operator in the loop
  • Roadmaps that start with automation before the process can survive Monday morning

Cuban is right that utilization wins. For RevOps, the people who make utilization real aren’t shipping another generic product. They’re the ones who clean the data, rewrite the process, then put agents in your account.

Custom isn’t a feature flag. It’s an embed.

Would you rather hire the systems person, DIY agents, or embed for six months?
