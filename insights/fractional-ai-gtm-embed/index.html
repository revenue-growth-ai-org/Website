Fractional AI GTM: who it’s for and what six months buys

A fractional AI GTM / RevOps embed that fixes the foundation before anyone ships agents.

Michael Bogart · CEO & Chief Revenue Architect, Revenue-Growth.AI · September 14, 2026

Most Series A–C revenue teams hear the same two pitches.

Build it yourself with LangGraph and a weekend warrior. Or hire a full-time systems leader and wait six months while the CRM stays broken.

Neither fits most RevOps seats.

What’s missing is a third path: a fractional AI GTM / RevOps embed that fixes the foundation before anyone ships agents. That’s the work. Not a seat. Not a demo theater.

Who this is for

This is for the VP or Director of Revenue Operations at a Series A–C B2B SaaS company doing roughly $5M–$50M ARR. Salesforce or HubSpot is the system of record. Forecast and pipeline numbers are arguments, not facts. Leadership wants “AI in GTM” and you own the blast radius if it fails.

Sometimes the Engineering Lead sits in the room too — the person who can read a Terraform plan and cares where data lives. When the conversation turns to a control plane in your own AWS account, that person matters. Until then, RevOps owns the problem.

You’re not looking for another Zapier cookbook. You’re choosing between DIY agent builds, a full-time hire, or something that gets you cleanup and process this quarter.

What six months buys

Six-month AI GTM / RevOps embed — cleanup, process, then agents. Platform included when it fits.

That’s the default line. It means a sequence, not a catalog of tools.

Cleanup

Fields that don’t mean anything. Stages that reps ignore. Ownership that jumps between AE, SDR, and CS with no handoff. Reports that require a human interpreter.

Cleanup is boring. It’s also why every agent project dies. You can’t automate a pipeline definition that three people disagree on.

Process

Once the data can hold a truth, you fix how work moves. What “qualified” means. Who owns forecast. When a deal advances. What gets logged versus what gets guessed.

Process is the difference between a dashboard and an operating system. Agents amplify whatever you have. Garbage process becomes garbage at scale.

Agents

Only then. Agents on a clean foundation can score, route, nudge, summarize, and surface risk without inventing reality. Agents on a messy CRM invent fiction faster than your team can catch it.

Proof, not a pitch deck

At AskNicely we ran this pattern. Lifted forecasted win rate 18% and doubled qualified pipeline in the same quarter.

Same CRM. No magic model. Cleanup and process first. Agents after. That’s the war story. The rest is noise.

How this is different

Most vendors lead with a platform or a seat. We don’t.

We lead with the embed. We sit with RevOps. We fix what’s broken. When a platform belongs in the picture — when you need the control of DIY, the speed of SaaS, and data that never leaves your AWS account — it’s included when it fits. When it doesn’t fit, we don’t force it into the pitch.

True competitors for this work:

  • DIY LangGraph / internal build — you own the stack, the maintenance, and the opportunity cost
  • Hiring a full-time systems headcount — right for some orgs, slow for most teams that need cleanup now

A fractional embed sits between those two. You get senior GTM systems work without waiting for a hire and without betting the year on an internal agent framework that never leaves staging.

What we don’t do

We don’t lead with “book a demo.” We don’t pretend a chatbot fixes stage hygiene. We don’t sell AI before the CRM can tell the truth.

If your first instinct is to pick a tool before you fix stages and ownership, you’re solving the wrong problem. Tool-first AI stalls. Foundation-first AI compounds.

The sharp question

If you’re between a DIY agent build and a full-time systems hire — what’s actually blocking you this quarter: dirty data, broken process, or both?
