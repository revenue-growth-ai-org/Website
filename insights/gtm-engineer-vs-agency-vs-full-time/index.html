GTM engineer vs agency vs full-time hire: who ships what

Compare what ships first, who owns it after, where agents run, and who sends.

Michael Bogart · CEO & Chief Revenue Architect, Revenue-Growth.ai · October 6, 2026

Short answer: Hire full-time when you have the time to recruit and a clean system to hand them. Use an agency when you want outbound volume and are fine renting it. Use an embed when the CRM and process need fixing before any agent is worth building, and you want your team to own the result.

The question is not "who". It's "what ships first."

Every option promises agents. The difference is what happens in the first weeks.

A full-time hire spends the first stretch learning your CRM. An agency spins up campaigns on the data you have. A DIY build puts an agent on top of the data you have.

Two of those three automate whatever is already in the CRM. If the CRM is wrong, the agent is wrong faster.

When a full-time hire is right

You already have clean data and a written process. You have time to recruit. You want one person who owns the system for years.

The risk: the system lives in one head. When they leave, so does the map.

When an agency is right

You want outbound volume and you're fine renting it. The work lives in their tools, and their people often send.

The risk: when the contract ends, the pipeline machine goes with it.

When a DIY build is right

Your team already runs Make, Zapier, or n8n, and the data is sound. You need one narrow agent, not a system.

The risk: an agent built on loose stage definitions and duplicate accounts drafts confident, wrong output. Nobody notices until a rep sends it.

When an embed is right

The CRM needs cleanup. The process isn't written down. You want agents, but you want your team to own them.

That's the order Revenue-Growth.ai runs in a six-month AI GTM / RevOps embed:

  1. Clean the CRM data.
  2. Fix the sales process.
  3. Build agents in your existing stack. They research and draft. Your people send.
