Build vs buy AI agents for RevOps: the agents are the easy part

The hard part is the AWS setup your security team will sign off on, and keeping it that way after launch.

The hard part is the safe AWS setup: IAM roles with a permissions boundary, KMS encryption in your account, the platform's AWS activity logged to your own CloudTrail, and every release version-pinned so we can go back to a known-good version. One teammate per person vs. a team of agents for the book

A personal assistant like Grok Bot is a teammate for one person. It drafts, researches and gets tasks done in that person's apps. A revenue agent platform is a team of agents for the whole revenue org. It runs on a schedule in your AWS account, works in your CRM, and keeps records and pipeline clean for everyone. The hard part: roles, permission limits, encryption, audit logs, locking

"Build it or rent it" is the argument everyone is having. It's the wrong first question. An agent that reads an Opportunity and drafts a next step is the quick part. Getting your security team to let it run every night across the whole CRM is the project.

Here's what the safe setup takes:

  • The agents run in your own AWS account, under your IAM roles.
  • The roles it creates carry a permissions boundary.
  • Data is encrypted with KMS keys in your account.
  • The platform's AWS activity logs to your own CloudTrail.
  • Changes are locked so only one runs at a time, and every release is version-pinned so we can go back to a known-good version.

Each of those decides what an agent can see versus what it's allowed to act on. None of them is the agent. We've built this setup and tested it in real AWS installs, including a live customer deployment.

How your security team reviews it

It runs in your account, so your team reviews it the way they review anything else there. The agents act under your IAM roles. Data is encrypted with KMS keys in your account. The platform's AWS activity logs to your own CloudTrail, so when a rep asks why a record moved, you can trace it through your CRM's change history and the platform's logs in your account, not someone's chat thread.

Don't grant write access by default. Start with what an agent can see. Let each workflow earn the fields it's allowed to change. Keep a human in the loop on anything that sends.

Who keeps it pinned, updated and audited after launch

Launch day is where the real work starts. Somebody has to keep every release pinned and know what changed, re-check permissions every time the CRM changes, trace changes through the CRM's change history and the platform's logs, and turn off the agents that aren't earning their keep. Building agents is code. Deciding which ones are worth running is RevOps judgment.

Build, buy, or embed: a short checklist

  • Build it yourself if you have someone who owns AWS IAM, KMS and CloudTrail, someone who owns RevOps process, and both will stay for as long as the agents run.
  • Buy a SaaS agent if your security team is fine with the agent running in the vendor's account instead of yours.
  • Embed if you want the agents in your account, under your controls, without hiring two people to get there. For how this compares with a full-time hire, see who ships what.

FAQ

Can AI agents run on my CRM inside my own AWS account?

Yes. The agents run in your own AWS account under your IAM roles. Data is encrypted with KMS keys in your account, and the platform's AWS activity logs to your own CloudTrail.

How does my security team review and control AI agents that write to your CRM?

With the controls they already use. The agents act under your IAM roles, and the roles it creates carry a permissions boundary. The platform's AWS activity logs to your own CloudTrail. Start read-only, and let each workflow earn write access one workflow at a time.
